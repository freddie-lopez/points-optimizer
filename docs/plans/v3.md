# Build plan: Live Trip Mode (v3)

**Author**: Architect
**Date**: 2026-09-08
**Baseline**: v1 shipped (345 tests). The Seats.aero parser fix shipped on top of
it (395 tests). **All 395 must stay green**; §8 names the only three that may
change and requires each to be justified in the run report.
**Scope chosen**: one feature. Live Trip Mode. Nothing else.

---

## 1. Summary

The tool has two modes and they cannot meet.

- `--trip-fixture` scores a whole multi-leg trip, but every price in it — cash
  *and* points — comes from screenshots Tsuki captured. It never calls
  Seats.aero. Not once.
- `--origin/--destination/--date` calls Seats.aero for real, and since the
  parser fix it correctly parses what comes back. It scores exactly one route.

So the headline number — Trip B beats cash by **6.55% – 15.66%** — rests on
Google Flights badges on all four flight legs. The parser fix confirmed one of
those badges (SFO→MAD, Aeroplan, 50,000 Y, 9 seats) against a real response and
explicitly declined to extend that confirmation to the other three. The v2 coder
report states the consequence plainly: *"The range would only move if Trip B were
re-run against live Seats.aero data for all four flight legs. That has not been
done and must not be implied."*

**That is the whole of v3.** The margin cannot become trustworthy until a whole
trip can be scored against live availability, and today there is no code path
that does it. v3 builds that path and nothing else.

The single sentence v3 must be able to say and v2 structurally cannot:

> **"Trip B beats cash by X% — 4 of 4 flight legs scored against live Seats.aero
> availability captured on 2026-09-08, cash from Tsuki's own captures, snapshots
> in `tests/fixtures/seats_aero/live_trip_b/`."**

And the sentence it must be equally able to say, because it will be the common
case:

> **"Trip B beats cash by X% — 3 of 4 flight legs live, 1 leg (B3) badge-derived
> because Seats.aero returned no award space on that date. This margin mixes two
> provenances and must not be quoted as a live number."**

v3 adds no new scoring logic, no new surcharge data, no new programs, no
productization. It is plumbing plus provenance.

## 2. Assumptions & decisions

### Settled — do not re-derive

- **The response schema is known.** Documented in
  `runs/points-optimizer/parser-fix-report.md`, fixtured at
  `tests/fixtures/seats_aero/sfo_mad_real.json`. Per-cabin `{X}Available` /
  `{X}MileageCost` / `{X}TotalTaxes` / `{X}RemainingSeats` / `{X}Airlines` /
  `{X}Direct`; `Source` nested at `Route.Source` as a lowercase code; taxes as
  **integer cents** in `TaxesCurrency`; clean fields, never `*Raw`. `src/seats_client.py`
  implements all of it and 54 tests pin it. **Do not touch the parser in v3**
  except where §6 Step 1 explicitly separates transport from parsing.
- **Seats.aero returns a candidate carrier LIST, not operating metal.**
  `"AC, LH, UA, VL"`. So `Award.has_known_metal` is False on most live rows and
  the v1 surcharge model resolves to UNKNOWN on most live legs. **This is
  expected behaviour, not a bug, and no v3 step may "fix" it by picking a
  carrier from the list.** `SurchargeTable.resolve_ambiguous_metal()` already
  handles the two honest outcomes (all carriers agree → that figure with the
  ambiguity disclosed; they differ → UNKNOWN with the per-carrier outcomes
  named).
- **The 1,000 calls/day Pro cap is real** and `SeatsClient.DAILY_CALL_CAP`
  already enforces it per calendar day across instances.
- **`data/bonuses.csv` stays empty. No card or balance is hardcoded. The wallet
  is runtime input.** Unchanged from v1.
- **Absence of a surcharge row means UNKNOWN, never $0.** Unchanged, and now
  load-bearing on a new axis: an *API* absence must not become a $0 either.

### New decisions for v3

- **The trip fixture owns the cash side. Live mode owns the points side.**
  There is no cash-price API in scope and there will not be one. Screenshots
  remain the source of truth for cash. Live mode replaces a leg's
  `points_candidates`, and touches `cash_options` never.
- **Cash provenance and points provenance are tracked and reported
  separately, per leg.** They are two independent facts about a leg and v2 has
  no vocabulary for the difference. Every leg result carries both.
- **Points provenance has three reported states and five internal ones.**
  Reported: `live` / `badge_fallback` / `unavailable`. Internally `unavailable`
  is always one of two distinct states that must never merge:
  `no_award_space` (the API answered, and the answer was "nothing") and
  `api_error` (we never got an answer). **This project has made exactly this
  mistake twice** — v0's phantom $0 surcharge and v2's broken parser reporting
  a real 9-seat award as "no award availability". The type system, not a
  convention, keeps them apart: see §4.3.
- **Only an award on the leg's own date may be scored against that leg's cash
  price.** A ±N-day flexibility window is supported and is useful, but an award
  found on Jan 17 is compared against nothing, because there is no Jan 17 cash
  price and the tool cannot invent one. Off-date awards are reported as a
  separate advisory block and **never enter the trip margin**. Reasoning and
  the direction of the bias this avoids: §4.5.
- **The disk cache stores raw responses, not parsed Awards.** A parser fix must
  be re-runnable against yesterday's responses without spending a single API
  call. The v2 experience is the argument: the parser was wrong for the entire
  life of the project and the fix was only possible because one raw response
  existed. Caching parsed objects would have cached the bug.
- **Every raw response that reaches the cache is also archived as a test
  fixture.** Requirement 4 and the cache write are the same event, so it cannot
  be skipped. The SFO→MAD capture became the first real-shape test data and
  immediately exposed a broken parser; every future response earns its place in
  the regression corpus the same way.
- **A leg with a known award cost and an unknown surcharge is reported as a
  floor plus a break-even, never as a blank.** The machinery already exists
  (`LegResult.points_floor_usd`, `break_even_surcharge_usd`,
  `surcharge_cannot_change_verdict`) and v1 proved it works — Trip A's A1 leg is
  a *confident* PAY CASH despite an UNKNOWN surcharge, because 43,000 points is
  $430 before any surcharge and cash is $367. v3's job is to make sure the live
  path always populates those fields and the formatter always prints them.
- **FX rates get sourced values and a date.** See §2.1. `--fx` still overrides
  everything.
- **Hotels stay manual.** No hotel award API exists. Seats.aero is flights-only.
  Unchanged from v1, restated in every hotel row of the output.

## 3. Out of scope for v3

Ruthlessly. This version does one thing.

- **A cash-price API, or any live cash pricing.** Not in scope now or later.
  Screenshots are the cash source of truth.
- **Hotel award search.** Still no API. Manual entry only.
- **Award chart modeling.** Still never computes what an award *should* cost.
- **Extending the surcharge table**, adding programs, adding `(program ×
  partner-metal)` rows, or closing the economy-cabin gap. All real work; all v4.
- **Multi-currency partner data** (MR / TY / C1 / Bilt rows). The engine exists
  and the table is still empty. Live mode does not need it.
- **Resolving operating metal.** v3 designs for its permanent absence. The
  trips-endpoint probe (§9.2) is a *possible* future input, not a v3 dependency.
- **Positioning flights.** MRY↔SFO/SJC stays unpriced input, as in v0/v1.
- **Government taxes and UK APD.** Still unmodelled, still disclosed. A `$0`
  carrier surcharge is not a `$0` ticket.
- User accounts, hosting, billing, GUI, scraping, async, concurrency.
- **Re-running Trip A.** Its two legs are a JetBlue round trip and a Marriott
  stay; live mode adds nothing.

### 4.2 The seam: live mode replaces candidates, not scoring

```
load_trip_fixture(path)  ->  TripFixture   [cash: captured, points: badges]
                                  |
                      live_trip.apply_live(fixture, client, opts)
                                  |
                                  v
                          TripFixture'                     per leg:
                                  |            cash_options   UNTOUCHED
                                  |            points_candidates REPLACED (or not)
                                  |            provenance     SET, always
                                  v
                        evaluate_trip(...)   <- unchanged v1 code
                                  |
                                  v
                        trip_totals(...)     <- gains provenance counts only
```

`apply_live` is a pure-ish transform: fixture in, fixture out, plus an outcome
record per leg. It is the only new place that knows about the network.

**Badge candidates are never silently discarded.** When a leg goes live, its
badge candidates move to `leg.superseded_candidates` and the formatter prints
one line per superseded badge saying what the badge claimed and what live found.
That is how a divergence between a Google badge and reality becomes visible
instead of vanishing — and given that exactly one of four badges has ever been
corroborated, divergence is the interesting case.

### 4.3 The outcome type — the anti-collapse mechanism

```python
class PointsProvenance(str, Enum):
    LIVE           = "live"            # API answered; scoreable awards found
    BADGE_FALLBACK = "badge_fallback"  # fixture badge used; live did not supply
    UNAVAILABLE    = "unavailable"     # no points side at all for this leg


class LiveQueryState(str, Enum):
    OK              = "ok"               # answered, >=1 award parsed
    NO_AWARD_SPACE  = "no_award_space"   # answered, 0 awards. A FINDING.
    API_ERROR       = "api_error"        # never answered. NOT a finding.
    NOT_QUERIED     = "not_queried"      # hotel leg, no IATA, or --live off
    BUDGET_EXHAUSTED = "budget_exhausted" # cap hit mid-trip. NOT a finding.


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class LiveLegOutcome:
    leg_id: str
    state: LiveQueryState
    provenance: PointsProvenance
    queried: Optional[LiveQuerySpec]     # origin, destination, window, cabins
    awards_parsed: int = 0
    awards_on_leg_date: int = 0
    awards_off_date: int = 0
    rows_seen: int = 0
    rows_skipped: int = 0
    error: str = ""                      # POPULATED IFF state is API_ERROR/BUDGET
    pagination_note: str = ""
    snapshot_path: Optional[Path] = None
    served_from_cache: bool = False
    cache_fetched_at: Optional[datetime] = None

    def __post_init__(self):
        # The invariant, enforced by construction, not by discipline.
        if self.state in (LiveQueryState.API_ERROR, LiveQueryState.BUDGET_EXHAUSTED):
            assert self.error, "an error state must say what the error was"
            assert self.awards_parsed == 0
        if self.state is LiveQueryState.NO_AWARD_SPACE:
            assert not self.error, (
                "NO_AWARD_SPACE means the API answered. An error here would mean "
                "an API failure is being reported as an absence of award space, "
                "which is the exact bug this project has now made twice."
            )
```

`NO_AWARD_SPACE` and `API_ERROR` render differently and always:

```
B3  AMS->LHR   points: unavailable   Seats.aero returned NO award space on
                                     2027-01-23 (searched 2027-01-23, 1 page,
                                     0 rows). This is a finding: there is no
                                     award to buy on this date.

B3  AMS->LHR   points: unavailable   Seats.aero COULD NOT BE REACHED
                                     (HTTPSConnectionPool timeout). This is an
                                     API FAILURE. NOTHING is known about award
                                     space on this leg. It is NOT a finding of
                                     no availability.
```

### 4.4 Response cache and the snapshot corpus

One canonical request key, one file, one snapshot:

```
key   = sha256("v1|search|SFO|MAD|2027-01-15|2027-01-15")   # canonical, sorted params
path  = data/cache/seats_aero/<key>.json
```

The envelope, which is also the fixture format:

```json
{
  "_meta": {
    "cache_schema": 1,
    "request": {"endpoint": "search", "origin_airport": "SFO", "...": "..."},
    "request_key": "<sha256>",
    "fetched_at": "2026-09-08T21:14:03Z",
    "fetched_by": "v3 live trip mode",
    "http_status": 200,
    "pages": 1,
    "pagination_note": "single page: the response carried NO count / hasMore / cursor ...",
    "key_redacted": true,
    "leg_id": "B3",
    "trip_id": "trip_b_europe"
  },
  "pages": [ { "data": [ ... ] } ]
}
```

- **Raw pages, verbatim, in order.** Not parsed Awards. A future parser fix
  replays them for free.
- **`Partner-Authorization` never appears.** The header is not part of the key,
  is not stored, and a test greps every cache and fixture file for the live key
  and for anything shaped like one.
- **TTL.** `CACHE_TTL_SECONDS` default **6 hours**. Award space moves; a
  day-old cache quietly re-answering a fresh question is its own kind of lie.
  An expired entry is *not deleted* — it is kept, and a run that would have used
  it says so and re-fetches. `--cache-ttl 0` disables reuse; `--refresh` forces a
  re-fetch and overwrites.
- **Invalidation is also by key.** The key includes `cache_schema` and the
  canonical parameter set, so changing the window, the date, or the request shape
  cannot hit a stale entry — it simply misses.
- **Every fetch writes a snapshot** into `--snapshot-dir` (default
  `tests/fixtures/seats_aero/live_trip_b/`), named
  `<leg_id>_<origin>_<destination>_<date>_<yyyymmddThhmmZ>.json`, identical
  envelope, deduplicated by content hash so a re-run does not litter. A
  `MANIFEST.md` in that directory is appended with one row per snapshot: leg,
  route, dates, fetched_at, rows, awards, state. **The manifest is what makes a
  live run reviewable after the fact**, and it is what a future
  `--from-snapshot <manifest>` replay would consume — designed for, not built in
  v3.
- The existing in-memory `SeatsClient.CACHE` and `clear_cache()` **stay exactly
  as they are** (four tests pin them). The disk cache sits *below* it, at the
  transport layer.

### 4.5 The flexibility window, and why an off-date award is not comparable

`--flex-days N`, default **0**. With N>0 a leg is queried
`start_date = leg.date - N`, `end_date = leg.date + N`, one call.

Then, and this is the whole design:

- Awards whose `Date` equals the leg date are **`date_match: exact`** and are
  the only ones that become `PointsCandidate`s. They are scored against the
  leg's cash price and enter the margin.
- Awards on any other date are **`date_match: offset(+2)`** and go into
  `LegResult.flexible_date_findings`. They are printed in their own block, with
  their own points price and taxes, and with the cash cell reading
  **`cash on 2027-01-17: NOT CAPTURED`**. They never touch a score, a verdict,
  or the trip margin.

The advisory block reads:

```
FLEXIBLE-DATE FINDINGS for B1 (searched +/-3 days; these are NOT scored)
  2027-01-13  Aeroplan  Y  35,000 + CAD 44.60   15 seats
     Cash for 2027-01-13 has NOT been captured. The $395 cash price on this
     leg is for 2027-01-15 and is not a comparison for this award. To score
     it, capture the cash fare for 2027-01-13 and add it to the fixture.
```

**Why not just score it against the Jan 15 cash price?** Because the comparison
would be systematically biased toward points, and the bias grows with N:

- A flexible search takes the **minimum over a window** on the points side while
  the cash side is a **single fixed draw**. Even with no correlation at all,
  min-of-(2N+1) versus one draw favours points.
- With correlation it gets worse in the same direction. Award space is released
  where demand is low; cash fares are low where demand is low. So the date the
  window picks is disproportionately a *cheap cash date* — meaning the true
  comparison price `cash(Jan 17)` is likely **below** the `cash(Jan 15)` the tool
  would have used. Scoring against the higher fixed price overstates what cash
  costs and hands points a margin it did not earn.
- And it is not only a pricing error. Moving B1 to Jan 13 moves the Madrid hotel
  check-in and shortens or breaks the connection to B2. That cost is real and is
  nowhere in the model.

There is exactly one honest way for a flexible award to enter the margin: **the
user captures the cash fare for that date and adds it to the fixture.** The
fixture schema gains a per-cash-option `date`, so a leg can carry cash prices for
several dates; an off-date award whose date has a captured cash price is promoted
to a scoreable candidate against *that* price, and the leg is reported as
date-shifted. Screenshots remain the source of truth for cash, which is the rule
this whole design is built around. This is the answer to open question §9.4.

### 4.6 Live award → PointsCandidate, and the taxes/surcharge trap

A parsed `Award` becomes a `PointsCandidate`:

| Award | PointsCandidate | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `program` | `program` | via `resolve_source`. `ur_transferable is False` → candidate kept and marked NO UR PATH; `None` → kept and marked unknown. Neither is dropped. |
| `cost` | `points` | clean field only |
| `award_type` | `cabin` | one candidate per available cabin |
| `candidate_carriers` | `operating_carrier` / `carrier_source` | **single-entry list only** → `operating_carrier=code`, `carrier_source="seats_aero"`. Multi-entry → `operating_carrier=""`, `carrier_source="seats_aero_ambiguous"`, which `has_known_metal` already reads as False. |
| `cash_component` + `cash_component_known` | see below | |
| — | `source` | `"seats_aero_live"` — a **new** provenance string, distinct from `google_badge_unverified` |
| — | `source_note` | carries the award's note verbatim, plus fetched_at and snapshot filename |

**The trap, stated so nobody walks into it.** `{X}TotalTaxes` is what the API
calls total taxes. The parser-fix report is explicit that *"it is not proof that
no carrier surcharge stacks on top for other programs."* So for a live award we
hold two facts that are easy to conflate: a **known** tax figure from the API,
and a **separately modeled** carrier surcharge whose relationship to that figure
is unobserved. Adding them risks double counting; ignoring the surcharge risks
undercounting — which is v0's bug.

The rule, and it is deliberately conservative:

- If the resolved surcharge is a **tier-5 program-policy $0** (United, Aeroplan,
  KrisFlyer on own metal — metal-independent by construction, per the v1 coder
  report §6.2), then there is no carrier surcharge to stack and `TotalTaxes` is
  taken as the complete carrier-side cash figure. The candidate is **scoreable**.
  This is the case the SFO→MAD capture actually falls into.
- Otherwise — a nonzero modeled band, or UNKNOWN — the tool **cannot tell**
  whether `TotalTaxes` already contains it. The candidate is **unscoreable**,
  and reports:
  - a **floor**: points at valuation + `TotalTaxes` converted, i.e. the least
    this can possibly cost;
  - a **break-even**: the additional surcharge above `TotalTaxes` at which
    points stop winning;
  - and the existing `surcharge_cannot_change_verdict` shortcut when the floor
    already loses to cash, so a certain answer is still delivered as certain.

A leg in that second state is **still useful** and must render as such. Never a
blank cell. §11.1 flags this rule as one of the three most likely to be wrong.

Note the happy consequence of the CAD rate (§2.1): the captured CAD 44.60 becomes
scoreable at $32.36 instead of being carried as an unknown, so the SFO→MAD leg
goes from "unconvertible tax, verdict robust from one direction only" to a fully
priced $532.36-vs-$395 PAY CASH.

### 4.7 The margin, with its provenance attached

`trip_totals` gains, and the formatter prints:

```
legs_flight_total          4
legs_points_live           3
legs_points_badge          1
legs_points_unavailable    0
legs_no_award_space        0
legs_api_error             0
margin_provenance          "mixed"        # "live" | "mixed" | "badge" | "none"
margin_provenance_note     "3 of 4 flight legs live; B3 badge-derived."
```

Rules:

1. **`margin_provenance == "live"` only if every flight leg with a points
   candidate came back `LIVE`.** One badge leg makes it `"mixed"`.
2. **The percentage may never be printed without its provenance line.** They are
   emitted by the same formatter call so they cannot be separated by accident.
   A single number that hides its own provenance is worse than no number.
3. `--require-all-live` **withholds the trip margin entirely** unless
   provenance is `"live"`, printing per-leg results and the reason for the
   withholding. For the moment someone wants to quote a number publicly.
4. The margin stays a **range** (`beat_cash_pct_low`/`_high`) exactly as v1
   built it. Live data narrows it where a program-policy $0 resolves; it will
   not collapse it, because most live legs will carry an UNKNOWN surcharge.

## 5. Tech choices

- **Python 3.11, dataclasses, stdlib `json`/`hashlib`, pytest, rich, requests.**
  Unchanged. Rejected again: async (four sequential calls; the cap, not latency,
  is the constraint), `requests-cache` (a general HTTP cache library would cache
  by URL including the auth header's request, and would not give us the snapshot
  envelope or the manifest for free — this cache is ~120 lines and is a
  *reviewable artifact format*, not a performance optimization), SQLite (one JSON
  file per request is diffable, greppable, and directly usable as a fixture;
  a database row is not).
- **Cache key = sha256 of a canonical parameter string.** Rejected a
  human-readable filename as the key: dates and windows make it ambiguous and
  filesystem-unsafe. The human-readable name lives on the *snapshot*, where
  browsability actually matters, and the manifest joins the two.
- **An `Enum` state plus a frozen dataclass with `__post_init__` assertions for
  query outcomes.** Rejected a `(list, error_string)` tuple — which is what the
  client does today via `last_error`, and which is precisely the shape that let
  "empty list" be reported as "no availability" for the entire life of the
  project. Rejected raising exceptions per leg: partial failure is a normal,
  expected outcome here, not an exception.
- **Snapshot-on-fetch, unconditionally.** Rejected a `--snapshot` opt-in flag:
  an archival step you can forget is an archival step that does not happen. The
  one real response this project owns exists because someone happened to paste
  it, and it immediately found a bug.
- **A separate `apply_live` transform rather than a `live=True` branch inside
  `evaluate_leg`.** Rejected the branch: `evaluate_leg` is 480 lines carrying
  every honesty invariant in the tool, pinned by ~200 tests. Live mode must not
  be able to change how a leg is scored — only what it is scored on.
- **TTL in seconds on the entry, checked at read.** Rejected a cache-sweep or
  eviction policy: nothing here outgrows a directory listing, and deleting a
  response is deleting evidence.

## 9. Open questions — flagged, not invented

**These are inputs. Nothing below may be guessed.**

1. **Balances and the full card list are still unsupplied.** Every run to date
   has used a synthetic `UR=180000` or no ceiling at all. **The stranding
   constraint — the most safety-critical rule in the tool — has never bound on
   real data.** Live mode does not need it to work, but a live margin computed
   against an unconstrained wallet is a margin for a wallet that may not exist.
   Step 10 should be run with the real numbers if they can be supplied, and the
   report must say which it used.

2. **The trips endpoint is unprobed.** `/partnerapi/trips/{id}` might return
   real segments and resolve operating metal, which would turn most UNKNOWN
   surcharges into figures and materially narrow the margin range. v3 is designed
   for its permanent absence (§2, §4.6) and Step 11 is explicitly non-blocking.
   **Do not assume metal will ever be available.**

3. **Pagination has never been observed.** The one capture was truncated at
   2,000 characters before any `count` / `hasMore` / `cursor` field. The client
   handles the documented shapes defensively, stops rather than inventing an
   offset scheme, and logs which path it took on every run. Step 10 is the first
   real chance to see a multi-page response — **the run report must state which
   pagination path each of the four legs took**, even if the answer is "single
   page, and another mechanism has not been ruled out".

4. **Should a flexibility window re-query the cash side too?** It cannot. There
   is no cash API and screenshots are the cash source of truth. §4.5 reasons this
   out; the short version: letting points shop across a window while cash stays
   pinned to one date takes a minimum on one side and a single draw on the other,
   and because award space is released on low-demand dates that also carry low
   cash fares, the fixed cash price is likely *higher* than the true comparison
   price. **The bias runs in favour of points**, and grows with N. v3 therefore
   refuses to score off-date awards and offers the honest escape hatch: capture
   the cash fare for that date. If Tsuki wants flexible-date comparisons to be
   scoreable, the input he needs to supply is cash prices per date, not a code
   change.

5. **Trip B's own data is still internally inconsistent** and was flagged in v0,
   v1 and v2 without being fixed. The fixture's B1 is **SFO**→MAD while its
   description says MRY→MAD, so the MRY positioning flight is unpriced and the
   live query will be for SFO. B3 is AMS→**LHR**. The Madrid and Amsterdam hotels
   both run Jan 15–19, which is impossible. Flights are priced for 1 adult and
   hotels for 2. **Live mode will query the airports the fixture names**, and a
   live margin computed over an incoherent itinerary is precise about the wrong
   trip. Confirm the four legs before Step 10 spends the calls.

6. **Which cabins should live mode search?** The API returns all four per row.
   v3 converts every available cabin to a candidate, so an economy trip may
   surface a business award that costs more and loses — noise, not a bug, but
   worth a `--cabin` filter if the output is cluttered. Default: keep them all
   and let the scorer decide.

## 10. Open risks

- **Live may simply return nothing for these routes and dates.** Trip B is
  ~16 months out, and cached award space that far ahead is thin and partner-
  dependent. The most likely outcome of Step 10 is a **mixed** margin, or one
  live leg and three empty. That is a real answer and the design treats it as
  one — but it means v3 can be fully, correctly built and still not produce the
  trustworthy live margin that motivated it. **The feature is the capability;
  the number is contingent on the world.**
- **Four calls per run makes it easy to burn the budget.** 1,000/day sounds like
  a lot until someone loops a `--flex-days` sweep. The cap is enforced, the
  budget is printed, and the cache is the real mitigation — but a careless
  `--refresh` in a loop defeats all three.
- **Snapshot drift.** A committed corpus that nobody replays rots. Step 2's
  "every snapshot parses into valid Awards" test is the guard, and it must run in
  CI, not by hand.
- **The taxes/surcharge relationship is unobserved** and §4.6's rule is a
  guess about a data semantics question. See §11.1.
- **The GBP correction moves the all-cash denominator**, so v3's badge-only
  margin will not exactly equal v2's 6.55%–15.66%. It must be reported as an FX
  effect and must not be confused with a live-data effect. A run report that
  conflates the two would be its own small version of the bug this project keeps
  making.
- **Provenance can be tracked and still not be read.** Every mechanism here is
  a label, and labels get quoted without their qualifiers. Rule 2 in §4.7 —
  percentage and provenance emitted by the same call — is the only structural
  defence, and it only defends the tool's own output, not a number someone
  copies out of it.
- **Scope creep back into the surcharge model.** Once live data is flowing, the
  pull to "just add an economy BA row" will be strong. That is v4. A v3 that also
  edits `surcharges.csv` is a v3 whose regressions cannot be attributed.

## 11. The three decisions most likely to be wrong

**1. The rule that `{X}TotalTaxes` is a complete carrier-side cash figure only
when the surcharge resolves to a program-policy $0.**
This is a guess about what Seats.aero's "total taxes" actually contains, made
from one captured row on one program that happens to levy no YQ. If the field in
fact always includes carrier surcharges, v3 needlessly withholds a score on most
live legs and the margin stays a wide range for no reason — the tool would be
honest and useless. If it never includes them, then even the program-policy $0
legs are understated, and the one number v3 does confidently score is wrong in
the same direction as v0's original bug. The right resolution is empirical: find
one live row for a program *known* to levy YQ (BA, VS, Flying Blue on partner
metal), compare `TotalTaxes` against a real booking page, and write the answer
down. Step 10 may produce that row by accident; if it does, this rule should be
revisited before anything is built on top of it.

**2. Refusing to score off-date awards at all.**
The reasoning in §4.5 is sound about the bias, but the behaviour may be so
unhelpful that it gets routed around. A user who sees "35,000 miles on Jan 13 vs
50,000 on Jan 15, not scored" will simply edit the fixture's date to Jan 13 and
re-run, which scores the cheap award against the Jan 15 cash price anyway — the
exact comparison the design refused to make, now with no advisory attached and
no record that a date was shifted. The guard would then be worse than useless:
it would have moved a disclosed unfairness into an undisclosed one. The
mitigation is that the promotion path (capture cash for that date) is cheap and
documented, and that `DATE_SHIFTED` is recorded when it is used — but if Step 10
shows flexible dates are where the real value is, the honest answer may be to
score them with a prominent, permanent "cash side not re-priced; this comparison
favours points" label rather than to withhold.

**3. A time-based TTL as the reproducibility mechanism.**
A 6-hour TTL makes the margin a function of *when* you ran the tool, which is
precisely the property requirement 2 wanted to remove. Two people running the
same command four hours apart get the same answer; seven hours apart they get
different answers with no signal that anything changed but the clock. The unit
that actually makes a result reproducible is a **run manifest** — an explicit,
committed set of snapshot files that a run can be replayed against — and v3
builds the manifest but treats it as documentation rather than as an input.
`--from-snapshot <manifest>` is a small feature and it may be the one that
matters most; it was left out to keep v3 to a single feature, and that may be
the wrong call. If Step 10's margin is going to be quoted anywhere, it should be
quoted against a manifest, not against a cache.
